Portrait of Terentius Neo & His Wife

Discovered in Pompeii (House VII, 2, 6), this famous 1st-century AD fresco portrays a prominent local baker and his wife holding symbols of literacy and high social status: a folded wax writing tablet (tabula cerata), a bone stylus, and a papyrus scroll (rotulus). The realistic portraiture reflects the civic pride and cultural aspirations of Pompeii's commercial elite.

Painted Roman Altar (Milan)

Housed in the Civico Museo Archeologico di Milano, this limestone votive altar showcases private Roman religious practices. It features vivid painted reliefs of Ceres (the goddess of harvest and fertility) and a winged Victory, complemented by a carved bearded drain spout for sacrificial libations and framed in classic Pompeian red borders.
